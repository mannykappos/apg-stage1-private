## 1. In plain words

The locked blueprint (Sections 00 to 18, version 1.0) already says most of what the data supply design needs: one intake with strict controls, nothing invented when data is missing, and warnings when data is stale. What it does not yet say is how APG makes sure it can always get the data, and how it tells the builder what it needs. This change request adds that.

- **What changes in the blueprint:** 59 additions to 7 of the 19 sections (01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 06 and 11), which go from version 1.0 to 1.1. Most of it goes into Section 04 (the intake): the ways of supplying data, the readiness record that tells the builder what is missing, the rule that every message says what to do, setup questions, and 14 new acceptance criteria (PG-DS-AC-01 to 14). Nothing already locked is reworded or removed; every change is an addition, and the full proposed wording is in section 12.
- **What it does not change:** no detection rule, no financial figure, no canonical financial definition (recognised margin, forecast final margin and the others keep their meaning), and no approval or control is weakened. Four additions make a control stricter (section 4).
- **What else it touches:** the parts of every section the change items bear on were read for knock-on effects: 679 existing requirements, listed by ID, in all 19 sections; none needs its wording changed. The package around the blueprint does need work after approval: the requirements matrix (59 new rows, and anchors that move), the terminology register (new terms), the manifest and hashes, and the package validator, which today rejects any section that is not version 1.0 (shown in section 6.7).
- **Three questions for you** (section 11): who approves the builder's severity levels; what "a percentage of the job" is a percentage of; and whether the main budget and profit include the approved forecast of what is left to spend.
- **Next:** an independent agent check of this assessment, fixes, then one GPT pass, then your formal approval. Only after approval are the version 1.1 files produced, and each later build still needs its own authorisation.

## 4. Does any locked meaning change?

Section 18 asks for extra care where a change touches a canonical financial definition, and the owner's standing rule is that anything that changes a claim's meaning, relaxes a condition or widens scope comes to him first. This is the item-by-item answer.

- **No canonical financial definition changes.** Recognised margin, forecast final margin, cash-position-adjusted margin, the authoritative forecast, the revenue lifecycle states, realised saving and source authority keep their locked meaning (each term in the terminology register is reviewed in section 6.6).
- **C02, the move to one intake, changes no text but changes results.** The locked Section 04 already requires reconciliation, authority and entity resolution for every record kind; stage 1 does not yet meet that for 24 kinds. When the move is built, the rules will see only reconciled records of those kinds, so some results will change. That is why it is its own change request, with a saved baseline of today's results and every difference explained and signed off (S04 DS025). PG-DS-AC-03 ("a new way in changes no rule") says in its own words that it does not govern this move.
- **C10 and C11 say how "approved" is met; they do not relax it.** S06 §5.1 requires thresholds "configurable within approved governance boundaries"; S06 §8 requires thresholds "documented, testable and approved". Defaults approved by the owner and accepted by the builder's approver, and severity levels the builder sets and that are then approved, both meet that. What is open is who approves the builder's severity levels (question 1). Findings shown ungraded until the levels are set is a labelled state, in line with S06 §8 (severity, urgency and confidence kept separate) and PG-AI-AC-08; it is not a default.
- **C13 adds no new revenue state.** S11 §8's "Disputed/rejected amount" stays one state; the builder's recorded outcome (disputed, at his own cost, resubmitted, withdrawn) is an attribute of a rejected variation, and S11 §11 already says APG records a dispute without judging it. The new view in S11 §8.2 shows existing states side by side and adds nothing into another state (S11 §8.1). The cost attribution in S03 §6.2 and S05 §6 groups a cost; it never counts it twice (PG-PC-AC-05 and S03 §6.4 still apply).
- **C14 follows the locked rules.** S05 §5.1 already requires one authoritative forecast, S05 §3.2 already forbids treating unapproved variations or disputed claims as secured revenue, S04 §9 already requires authoritative and provisional outcomes to be compared "without confusing their status", and S10 §7.1 isolates scenarios. The addition says how they are shown. Question 3 confirms that this is what the owner means.
- **Four additions make a control stricter, not looser:** a value keyed from a document needs a second person's confirmation (S04 §18 asks only that manual entry not bypass approval); email never sets the organisation from the address alone; a held record is corrected only through the review workflow; and a backdated entry never rewrites a result already shown.
- **Scope widens, as the owner directed.** Section 01 discovery gains the data supply questions (C16), and Section 04 gains the readiness record and the message rule. These are the owner's own directions of 2026-11-11 and 12, approved in principle on 2026-11-12, and this assessment is the step Section 18 requires before they are formally approved.

## 11. Questions for the owner

**Question 1. Who approves the builder's severity levels?** You decided the builder sets them at setup. Every other setting in APG is entered by one person and approved by a second before it counts; stage 1's code already refuses an approval by the person who entered the setting (finding F-67). For example, the office manager enters "high = $50,000 or 2% of the job" and the director approves it. A builder with only one person in APG could not get his levels approved under that rule. Claude's suggestion: keep the same two-person approval, and until the levels are approved, findings show marked "grading not set up yet". Letting one person enter and approve his own levels would loosen a control the code has today, so it needs your yes.

**Question 2. A percentage of what?** "2% of the job" needs a base. Claude's suggestion: the current approved contract value, that is the original contract plus approved variations. For example, on a $5,000,000 job with $100,000 of approved variations, 2% is $102,000. The other bases the code could use are the forecast final cost or the forecast profit.

**Question 3. What does the main budget and profit include?** You said the main figure counts only what is 100% verified. Part of any job's profit figure is the forecast of what is still to be spent, which is an estimate the builder approves, not an invoice. For example: contract $5,000,000; spent and invoiced $3,000,000; the approved forecast to finish $1,500,000; so the main profit is $500,000, labelled as a forecast. Beside it: a variation waiting for approval, +$80,000; a disputed claim, −$20,000. Claude's suggestion: the main figure uses only approved and verified records plus the approved forecast to finish, clearly labelled as a forecast, and everything not final sits beside it. Is that what you mean?

## Appendix B. Retention event (E77): proposed field definition

Taken from the stage 1 code (`fin/cash_states.py`: `record_event`, `check_event`, `fold`, `events_of`). Today the event is written straight to the store; under C18 it comes through the intake from the release A entry form.

| Field | Supplied by | Meaning and rule today |
| --- | --- | --- |
| project_ref | the person | the project whose retention pool moves |
| from_state, to_state | the person | one of the allowed moves: withheld to claimed, claimed to approved or disputed, approved to released or disputed, disputed to claimed or approved, released to received |
| amount_cents | the person | a positive whole number of cents, no more than the pool it leaves holds |
| evidence_ref | the person | required; for "received", a stored bank transaction of exactly this amount |
| note | the person | optional text |
| seq | APG | the next number in the project's sequence; events are append-only |
| actor, actor_kind, actor_roles | APG, from the signed-in person | a named person, never a service; "approved", "released" and "received" need the retention approver role, the others the clerk or approver role |
| at | APG | when it was recorded |

Two points the field definition must settle when the form is designed: the event has no currency of its own (it assumes the project's currency, where S03 §11.1 asks for currency to be explicit), and it moves a project-level pool, with no link to a particular contract or claim.

## Appendix C. The cost of extra work: proposed data design

- **What is new:** the cost lines that can carry an extra's cost (supplier invoice lines, E23; timesheet entries, E57, priced with the labour rate, E59; plant hire, E64) gain an optional reference to the instruction (E10) or variation (E09) the cost was incurred for.
- **One line, one place:** a cost line is attributed to at most one instruction or variation, or split between them by amounts that add up to the line; whatever is not attributed stays with the original scope. The cost is counted once in actual cost or commitments (S05 DS002).
- **Who sets it:** the person who supplies the line, or the person who later attributes it through the review workflow; a later attribution is a governed change, recorded with who, when and why (S04 §13).
- **What the extras view shows:** for each extra or variation, its value in each commercial state (S11 §8), its status, the cost attributed so far, and its effect on the job's profit; the original scope's budget and profit on their own.
- **Not decided here:** how an accrual or overhead allocation is shared with an extra (release B data), and how a builder's own system marks such costs (discovery, with sample exports).
